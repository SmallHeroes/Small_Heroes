## Pilot-specific anti-copy constraints

The next-generation Koko pilot may not use:

- a bed, bedroom, first night in a room, sleepover, or falling-asleep problem;
- moving boxes or placing one object from an old home as the solution;
- “a color from home” as a therapeutic symbol;
- any page order, payoff object, or prose from V3/V5 drafts;
- camouflage/disappearance as belonging;
- a story where קִים already knows the correct way to adapt.

It must use bedtime as an energy curve: active comic escalation through beat 5, then fewer choices, smaller movement, and an earned safe landing in beats 6–8.

## Finalist A — היום שנכנס לתיק של קים

### The promise

At closing time, קִים opens her mustard satchel and all the daylight she accidentally packed spills into the wrong objects. A banana becomes a lamp, a streetlight turns lemon-yellow, and pigeons announce morning again. {{childName}} must light the river's final lantern so the paper-boat parade can begin.

### Why this is Koko-only

The mess comes from her exact flaw: she catalogued and packed the day's colors instead of participating in the change. Her eyes choose different matches, her tongue applies a third route tag, and her old labels make the mistake spread. A generic companion cannot power the sorting comedy or the final reversal.

### Physical play and failures

- **Play system:** follow escaped colors, observe what each wrong color makes an object do, and route it using tangible tags.
- **Failure 1:** they sort by market-stall tags, but the tags also changed color; a tram collects cabbages as passengers.
- **Failure 2:** they try to restore every color to its old source, but the river lantern rejects them because night needs a color that did not exist during the day.
- **Comic rule of three:** glowing banana/pigeons → tongue stuck to a sign/tram full of cabbages → several colors escape to the bridge while the final lantern refuses all old labels.

### Set pieces

1. Closing market: the spill and first mismatches.
2. Evening tram: wrong labels physically change the route.
3. River bridge and boat lantern: one focused choice and visible release.

### Child-owned climax

The child notices a new blue formed by tram-shadow over river water, removes the old tags, creates a new two-part marker, and directs the final color. קִים follows the child's system; she does not reveal it.

### Ending and reread value

The lantern lights in the newly made blue. The boats drift in one soft line and the pigeons finally stop reopening morning. On reread, children can spot colors sitting on the wrong future objects before the characters do.

### Risks

- Lock four hero colors; do not create uncontrolled rainbow continuity.
- Keep the tram and boat parade graphically simple.
- Koko's body remains harmonious green, never patchwork.
- The new color must be a practical solution, not an identity speech.

## Finalist B — תחנת האוטובוס שקמה והלכה

### The promise

Before the last little bus arrives, its stop pulls its pole out of the pavement and walks away on bench legs to find a better street. {{childName}} promised to be the first passenger at the new stop and must help it settle before the bus completes its evening loop.

### Why this is Koko-only

קִים repeatedly matches the stop to old route colors from her satchel. Her eyes track map and runaway bench in opposite directions, while her tongue punches the wrong tickets. The child's success depends on rejecting her visual match and creating a new public behavior.

### Physical play and failures

- **Play system:** plant the stop pole, wait for the route signal, inspect who forms a queue, and move the bench using street clues.
- **Failure 1:** an old red curb leads them to the bakery; loaves, pigeons, and carts form a ridiculous queue.
- **Failure 2:** the old map places the stop on the wrong side of the bridge; toy boats arrive instead of the bus.
- **Comic rule of three:** walking bench/spinning sign → six loaves seated in line and Koko punches her own tail → an entire wrong queue rides on the wandering bench.

### Set pieces

1. Old street: the stop uproots itself.
2. Bakery square and fountain: wrong queues multiply.
3. River bridge: the child constructs the new stop behavior.

### Child-owned climax

The child recognizes where route, light, and actual passengers meet, draws a waiting line, invites the passengers into it, and turns the bench toward the road. A place becomes a stop through the child's action, not through an old token.

### Ending and reread value

The round bus stops exactly at the chalk line. The bench plants its foot and the first ride rolls quietly along the river. The queue jokes and small route clues reward anticipation on reread.

### Risks

- Give the stop one consistent, readable physical design.
- Limit wrong queues to three simple groups.
- Do not also anthropomorphize the bus; the stop is the hero object.
- Keep the social act practical, not a belonging lesson.

## Finalist C — תחנת הדלתות של בין הערביים

### The promise

At twilight, every door in the street steps off its hinges and queues to change address. The destination tags stick to Koko's tongue, so the bakery door opens into the aquarium and the theater door opens onto a roof. {{childName}} carries the missing paper figure for the final shadow show and must reach the correct stage.

### Why this is Koko-only

Koko's color memory is useful but incomplete. She matches doors to their old building colors, while her split eyes and sticky tongue keep transferring tags. The child combines her remembered colors with newly observed use-clues; both characters are necessary, but only the child designs the answer.

### Physical play and failures

- **Play system:** open one door, retrieve a physical clue, compare handle/sound/use, and attach a new address tag.
- **Failure 1:** ordering by old colors sends the theater door to the bakery and fills the stage with flour.
- **Failure 2:** testing doors in sequence makes each opening swap two more tags; the paper figure is nearly soaked and blown away.
- **Comic rule of three:** tags stuck to Koko's tongue → Koko exits with bread on her head → the aquarium door opens on the roof and paper fish circle the address tags.

### Set pieces

1. Door station: the rule and lost tags.
2. Bakery and aquarium: wrong-use consequences.
3. Roof and theater: evidence discovery and quiet final show.

### Child-owned climax

The child notices flour, salt, and chalk in the hinges, then makes two-part tags: one remembered color from Koko and one current-use clue. The doors click into their new places because of the child's combined system.

### Ending and reread value

The final door opens onto the correct stage, and the child's paper figure casts the last shadow as the street darkens. Door clues can be hidden in earlier illustrations for reread discovery.

### Risks

- Lock three hero doors; avoid a crowd of unique door designs.
- Explain the door rule through action, never narrator exposition.
- Keep theater audience minimal or offscreen.
- This finalist needs the most careful spine before prose.

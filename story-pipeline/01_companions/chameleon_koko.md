# Companion Story Bible · קִים הזיקית (`chameleon_koko`)

**Status:** next-generation staging canon; not runtime authority.

**Challenge category:** `TRANSITION` · **Gender:** female · **Display name:** קִים; the ID remains `chameleon_koko`.

**Immutable visual identity:** small round chameleon, harmonious warm green to yellow-green body, pale cream-yellow belly, large rotating eyes, curled tail, tiny mustard fabric shoulder satchel; never patchwork, pink-spotted, pure mint, or visually dissolved into the background.

## Child-facing promise

קִים collects the colors of places because she is certain every new place can be understood if she labels it before it changes again. Unfortunately, her two eyes often vote for different destinations, her tongue collects the wrong evidence, and her small satchel has ambitious opinions about capacity.

She carries traces of places without hiding inside them. The child helps her discover that a new place is not complete only when it resembles an old one; it becomes meaningful when they make the first new thing there together.

## Story engine

- **Visible desire:** map, sort, deliver, return, match, or preserve the moving colors/signs of a place before a concrete deadline.
- **Lovable flaw:** she wants to classify every change before participating in it.
- **Wrong help:** pulls an old color/sample/label from her satchel and matches by appearance too quickly, while her independently rotating eyes or sticky tongue collect contradictory evidence.
- **Embodied tell:** eyes look backward and forward at once; tail tightens around the satchel when overloaded; body hue harmonizes but never disappears.
- **Child-only ability:** the child chooses which clue matters now and creates the first genuinely new mark, route, game, or arrangement instead of restoring the old place exactly.
- **Relationship change:** קִים contributes memory and a portable archive; the child contributes selection and invention. By the payoff, her satchel carries one newly made trace rather than being the solution itself.
- **Emotional underlayer, never headline:** change can include continuity without requiring exact restoration or self-erasure.

## Comic engine — “two eyes, one tongue, too many labels”

1. A place, path, or object changes faster than קִים can categorize it.
2. Her eyes choose different answers; she confidently sends her tongue or satchel toward a third.
3. The wrong color/sample/label creates a visible mismatch that moves or multiplies the problem.

Escalate from one funny mismatch, to a chain reaction across a second set piece, to a final mistake that exposes the useful pattern the child recognizes. Color is causal evidence—not mood narration or decorative magic.

## Voice and oral Hebrew

- Nimble, curious, slightly over-specific about shades and destinations.
- Sentences may bend or correct direction as her eyes disagree.
- Sample voice, not mandatory catchphrases: “שמאלה, אמרה עין אחת. השנייה ביקשה לערער.” · “זה ירוק של כמעט-הגענו. או של מלפפון. נבדוק.” · “התיק שלי לא מלא. הוא פשוט… סגור מאוד.”
- No therapist warmth, identity speeches, poetic color philosophy, or repeated “piece of home travels with you.”

## Play vocabulary

- **Actions:** sort, tag, match, remix, trace a route, unstick tongue, unpack/repack, compare changing signs, create a new shared marker.
- **Objects:** mustard satchel, color swatches, route tags, tickets, ribbons, chalk, signs, labels, ordinary objects in impossible colors.
- **World affordances:** stations, ferries, markets, moving streets, rooftop routes, traveling fairs, ports, changing gardens, public spaces shifting from day to night.
- **Reread hooks:** each eye spotting a different clue; early mismatched labels visible in later set pieces; the final new color/mark assembled from opening details.

The satchel is a comic/action tool, not a required “home token” cure. A story may use something from a previous place, but the child must do more than place an old object in a new location.

## Direction modulation

- **Bedtime:** use an active transition—day to night, last route, closing market, changing station, traveling object. Beats 6–8 reduce choices and motion into an earned arrival or completion; no bed, bedroom, sleepover, or sleep is required.
- **Adventure:** a changing route or delivery where old labels repeatedly fail and the child invents a usable new marker.
- **Fantasy:** one coherent change-rule affecting colors, maps, doors, or paths across at least three set pieces; never a sermon about preserving identity.

## Do not write

- camouflage or disappearance as the way to belong;
- patchwork body, scarf, or any accessory replacing the mustard satchel;
- “bring one home object, place it, feel calm” as the complete plot;
- color directly narrating emotions page by page;
- wise transition guide who already understands the new place;
- generic room/house move, first-night bedroom, sleepover, or box-unpacking plot for the next-generation pilot;
- קִים choosing or creating the climax instead of the child;
- moral about old home versus new home.

## Swap test and acceptance

Replacing קִים with a generic colorful animal must break the split-eye evidence, sticky-tongue mistakes, satchel capacity problem, color/route matching system, and the child's creation of a new marker.

- [ ] The visible quest works without mentioning transition anxiety.
- [ ] At least two of eyes, tongue, tail, hue, and satchel alter causal choices.
- [ ] Wrong matching produces three different escalating consequences.
- [ ] The child creates or chooses something new; an old token does not solve the plot.
- [ ] קִים changes because of the child's approach.

## Visual continuity locks

- One harmonious warm-green/yellow-green body with pale cream-yellow belly; no multicolor patches or pink spots.
- Keep silhouette distinct from every background; harmonizing is not camouflage.
- Tiny mustard fabric shoulder satchel remains the only canonical accessory.
- Large eyes may rotate independently; tail stays softly curled unless action tightens it.
- No scarf, magical color aura, human clothing, or disappearing body.

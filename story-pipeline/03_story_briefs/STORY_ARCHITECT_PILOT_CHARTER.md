# Small Heroes — Story Architect Pilot v2

## Stage 1: invent the story before writing it

Create exactly three story shapes that are genuinely different stories, not alternate wording or swapped props.

Across A, B and C, vary all of the following:

- the central comic engine;
- how Dini's inner character becomes action;
- the kinds of obstacles and visual movement;
- how the child intentionally affects or investigates the story before the climax;
- the principle of the child's climactic action; and
- the flavor of the payoff.

At least one option should take a bold, surprising direction while remaining coherent and child-friendly. Do not inherit choreography, materials, body-part business, transport devices, obstacle order, discoveries or solutions from an earlier draft or editorial brief. No specific maneuver is mandatory. Never use the same manifestation of Dini's personality in more than one option.

Every shape must offer these functions without prescribing their implementation:

- an immediately visible, child-interesting disruption that can carry the opening spread;
- enough causal room for one memorable large visual-comedy escalation in addition to smaller moments;
- a meaningful pre-climax action initiated by the child: an experiment, attempt, comparison, manipulation, choice, investigation, negotiation or another story-specific contribution;
- materially different dramatic problems rather than the same failure in new scenery; and
- one significant idea that cannot be directly predicted from the Creative Nucleus.

Each shape must contain only:

1. `Title` — a provisional Hebrew title;
2. `Hook` — the opening situation in one or two sentences;
3. `Comic engine` — what keeps generating new, varied fun;
4. `Journey shape` — three or four broad changes of situation, not pages and not a beat-by-beat synopsis;
5. `Child agency arc` — how the child actively changes the story before and during the climax, without prescribing exact actions;
6. `Climax principle` — what kind of decisive action the child leads, without choreographing every move;
7. `Payoff flavor` — the visual/emotional promise of the ending without writing the ending;
8. `Why Dini` — why this could not work unchanged with a generic companion; and
9. `Surprise` — the element neither the title nor the initial premise makes obvious.

Do not write prose, dialogue, page numbers, image directions, exact recurring gags, production metadata or a complete solution sequence. Do not recommend a winner and do not converge the options toward one hidden preferred plot. End with exactly `WAITING_FOR_GUY_SELECTION`.

## Stage 2: only after Guy selects A, B or C

Treat the selected shape as the chosen story direction, not a screenplay. Do not blend rejected options into it. Write exactly the declared number of Hebrew text pages with lively read-aloud prose. Invent transitions, dialogue, local action, comic timing and details freely.

Four drafting priorities only:

1. enter the strange or funny physical event quickly;
2. for adventure, create one memorable large visual-comedy escalation;
3. before the climax, let the child initiate one meaningful story-specific action that tests, changes or advances the situation; and
4. when consecutive pages perform the same dramatic job, compress one or transform its function.

These are story functions, not mandatory events. Return only minimal identity frontmatter and numbered prose pages; do not include analysis, world rules, recurring-gag labels or image directions. End with the strongest story beat, whether funny, warm, quiet or surprising; do not force a lyrical final sentence.

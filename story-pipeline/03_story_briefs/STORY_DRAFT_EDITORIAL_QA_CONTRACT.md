# Small Heroes — Post-Draft Editorial QA Contract v1

**Status:** staging-only review authority. This document is never supplied to the Story Architect or first-draft Writer.

The editor reviews a completed draft without rewriting it into compliance by force. The result is `pass`, `revise` or `reject`, with concise evidence tied to the draft.

## Story QA

- The child has a concrete want and materially changes what happens.
- Cause and effect remain understandable without explanatory moral language.
- The middle creates genuine escalation and variation rather than repeating one obstacle with new props.
- The climax is earned by earlier setup and led by the child.
- The ending pays off the story's central play, not merely its lesson.

## Delight QA

- The draft contains multiple distinct moments a child could find funny, surprising or pleasurably tense.
- At least one scene makes a reader want to see the illustration.
- Language may become memorable through rhythm, image or surprise; a catchphrase is never mandatory.
- The story offers a reason to reread beyond personalization alone.

## Companion QA

- The companion is recognizable through choices and reactions, not repeated slogans or fixed choreography.
- The companion's strength and lovable limitation both affect the plot.
- The companion is indispensable but does not solve the child's climax.
- The companion can listen, adapt, laugh or change direction.

## Hebrew read-aloud QA

- Hebrew sounds written rather than translated or generated from a template.
- Sentence length, rhythm and page endings vary naturally.
- Vocabulary is child-readable in context without flattening the prose.
- Dialogue belongs to the speaker and avoids therapy, instructional or specification language.

## Category and visual-journey QA

- Bedtime resolves toward calm without becoming inert.
- Adventure sustains physical momentum and meaningful changes of place or situation.
- Fantasy delivers wonder with understandable internal logic.
- The sequence offers materially different illustration opportunities and avoids a book of near-duplicate compositions.

## Boundaries

- Do not repair a weak draft by inserting checklist phrases.
- Do not reward mechanical compliance when the story is dull.
- Do not reject an original choice merely because it differs from an earlier editorial beat sheet.
- Guy retains final product and story acceptance.
